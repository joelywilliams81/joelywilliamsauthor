Even the Spider Keeps Record

Poems, revised edition

31 poems · 50 pages · 5.5 x 8.5 in

Even the Spider Keeps Record is a collection of thirty-one poems about the keeping - the margin notes, the saved envelopes, the names repeated until they held. Joely Williams writes grief that does not ask permission and joy that arrives anyway: a childhood corner, a storm survived, a headline that would not leave, the small wreckage of ordinary days.

These are poems for anyone who has ever kept a record nobody asked them to keep. For the ones rebuilding in the same ruined corner, every time the web gets swept down.

Thirty-one poems. One web. Nothing lost on purpose.

Put the Phone Down, We Have Work to Do

Book One: A Journal for Healing What People Broke

14 chapters · 324 pages · 7.57 x 9.94 in

Put the phone down. That is the whole first instruction, and most days it is the hardest one in this book.

Book One is a journal for healing what people broke. Fourteen chapters, one for each kind of wound a person can press into you: abuse, neglect, childhood abuse and neglect, witnessing violence, bullying, betrayal, separation, divorce, breakup, familial abandonment, social isolation, cult-related trauma, grief and loss, and the incarceration of a loved one.

Every chapter opens two ways in. Moments to Ponder asks you to sit inside a question until it tells you something true. Acts of Courage asks you to move - write it, draw it, mail it, burn it, say it out loud to a room that is empty except for you.

Skip the chapters that do not belong to your body. Linger in the ones that do. This journal is a tool for personal reflection, not a substitute for professional mental health care. Something greater than you already knows every page before you write on it, and is in no hurry for you to finish.

Fourteen chapters. Two doors in each. No rush.

Put the Phone Down, We Have Work to Do

Book Two: A Journal for Healing What Circumstance Broke

15 chapters · 351 pages · 7.57 x 9.94 in

Put the phone down. That instruction still comes first, and it still costs the most.

Book Two is a journal for healing what circumstance broke - the injuries no single hand delivered. The collision on the interstate. The diagnosis read aloud in a beige room. The eviction notice taped to the door. The border crossed with one suitcase. The paycheck that stopped. Illness. War. Debt. Systems that measured you and wrote the number down.

Fifteen chapters, each opening two ways in. Moments to Ponder asks you to sit inside a question until it tells you something true. Acts of Courage asks you to move - write it, map it, mail it, rebuild one small thing with your own two hands.

Begin where your body points. Leave the chapters that belong to someone else. This journal is a tool for personal reflection, not a substitute for professional mental health care. Something greater than you already knows every page before you write on it, and is in no hurry for you to finish.

Fifteen chapters. Two doors in each. No rush.
